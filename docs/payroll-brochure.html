Pay frequency is not a label on a report. It sets how income is annualised for PAYE, how far the UIF ceiling is scaled down, how the ETI bands are measured, and how much of a monthly loan instalment may be recovered. Get it wrong and the payslip still looks perfectly ordinary — which is why it is handled once, in one place, for both modules.

Calculated to the letter of the schedule

Tax engine

Every figure is driven by the SARS parameters for the year of assessment — brackets, rebates, thresholds and credits are data, not code, so a Budget change is a table update rather than a release.

PAYE

The averaging method, properly applied

Period income is annualised, taxed on the brackets, reduced by the age rebates and the annual medical-aid credits, then divided back to the period. Age 65 and 75 rebates apply automatically from the date of birth on the HR profile.

Section 11F

Retirement capped, not guessed

Pension, provident and RA contributions reduce taxable income only to 27.5% of the greater of remuneration or taxable income, and only to the annual cap pro-rated to the period. The employee still pays the full contribution; the excess is simply taxed.

Allowances & benefits

Travel at 80%, fringe benefits at cost

Only the taxable portion of a travel allowance is subject to PAYE. Taxable employer contributions raise the PAYE base as a fringe benefit without touching gross or net — the distinction most spreadsheets quietly lose.

Once-off amounts

Bonuses at the marginal rate

A 13th cheque, retro adjustment or leave pay-out is taxed as the difference between the annual tax with and without it — not annualised as though it recurred every month. Severance runs on the lump-sum table with its own tax-free band.

UIF & SDL

Ceilings and thresholds that follow the cycle

UIF is 1% employee and 1% employer against the statutory ceiling scaled to the pay period. SDL is levied only once annualised payroll crosses the threshold, so a small employer is not charged a levy it does not owe.

ETI

The incentive, where it is genuinely due

Employment Tax Incentive for qualifying employees aged 18–29, banded by monthly remuneration and months employed, tapering to nil at the ceiling. Non-monthly wages are restated to their monthly equivalent before the bands are applied.

Controls, because it is other people's money

Governance

Payroll is usually the largest payment a company makes and the one nobody outside it can check. The module is built so that every figure has an owner, a trail and a second pair of eyes.

Approvals

A chain you configure

Steps by role or by named person, with submit, approve, reject, comment, escalate and request-correction. A run cannot be released until the chain is satisfied.

Corrections

Amendments on the record

Corrections are suggested, approved and applied as their own tracked event rather than an untraceable edit to a released figure.

Change monitoring

Risk-scored master-data changes

Banking details, salaries and status changes are logged, scored for risk and surfaced for review — the changes that matter most are the ones made quietly between runs.

Assurance checks

Ghost employees, leakage, anomalies

Automated sweeps for duplicate banking details and unworked staff, for payments drifting outside their pattern, and for cross-checks between payroll, HR and the ledger.

Payslips

Password-protected, stored, delivered

Each payslip is rendered to an encrypted PDF locked with the employee's own key, filed against their profile, emailed and notified in-app. Employees retrieve their own history from self-service.

Audit

Every run, every action

Creation, calculation, locking, release and every approval decision are written to the payroll audit log with the user, the time and the totals as they stood.

The numbers do not stop at the payslip

Integration
  • General ledger. Releasing a run posts the salary journal to Finance through the posting outbox — a failure there is queued for retry and never unwinds a released payroll.
  • Departmental budgets. The run's cost is reported to the budget module split by department, idempotently, so a re-release cannot charge a budget twice. Until this existed, budgets only ever heard about purchase orders.
  • Leave. Leave is synchronised into the run, so unpaid days and pay-outs reach the payslip without re-keying.
  • Loans and advances. Active staff loans and disbursed salary advances are recovered automatically, scaled to the pay period and matched to the payslip's own month.
  • Banking. Runs generate ACB-format bank files for the employer's bank, or disburse directly through a connected payout provider with status tracked by webhook.
